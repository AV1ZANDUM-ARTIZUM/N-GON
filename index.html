Welcome to the Learning Center

Physics describes how objects move, interact, and change. Use the sections below as a quick reference while studying. Try predicting an outcome before checking an equation or running an experiment—that is an important part of scientific thinking.

Motion

Velocity tells you how quickly position changes and in what direction. Acceleration describes how velocity changes over time.

v = Δx / Δt
a = Δv / Δt

Forces

A net force changes an object's motion. Mass measures resistance to acceleration.

F = m × a

Common forces include gravity, friction, tension, and the normal force.

Gravity

Near Earth's surface, falling objects accelerate downward at roughly 9.8 m/s² when air resistance is ignored.

W = m × g

Weight is a force; mass is not.

Energy

Energy can move between forms and systems. Mechanical examples include kinetic and gravitational potential energy.

KE = ½mv²
PE = mgh

Momentum

Momentum depends on both mass and velocity. In an isolated system, total momentum is conserved.

p = m × v

Scientific Thinking

Make a prediction, identify variables, collect observations, compare results, and explain what the evidence suggests.

Experimental Thinking

Try this: Predict how changing mass, force, or surface conditions might affect an object's motion. Then test one variable at a time and record what happens.

Ask: What changed? What stayed constant? Does the result match the model?
