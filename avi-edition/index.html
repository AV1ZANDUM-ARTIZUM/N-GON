A concise guide to motion, forces, gravity, energy, and experimental thinking.

Motion

Velocity describes how position changes over time. Acceleration describes how velocity changes.

Forces

Forces can change an object's motion. Net force determines the direction of acceleration.

Gravity

Gravity is an attractive force between masses and plays a central role in orbital and falling motion.

Key relationships

For constant acceleration, several useful relationships connect position, velocity, acceleration, and time.

F = m × a
v = v₀ + a × t
Δx = v₀t + ½at²

Good experiments change one variable at a time, measure the result, and compare the observation with a prediction.

Experimental thinking

Observe → Predict → Test → Measure → Explain. Interactive simulations can help visualize concepts that are difficult to observe directly, but the same scientific reasoning still applies.
